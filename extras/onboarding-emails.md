# Portafile — Onboarding Email Sequence (5 emails)

Voice rules applied: money-and-mornings concrete, no "empower/journey/seamless," numbers over adjectives. Sent as a **calendar drip** to founding shops who opted in by email — the app itself reports nothing back (nothing is uploaded, by design), so the day-N schedule and "if" notes below are a manual sending guide for Ethan, not tracked automation.

---

## Email 1 — day 0 (welcome)
**Subject: Your 15 cards are ready (they're guesses — fix one)**

You just opened Portafile. Fifteen recipe cards are already in there, priced with *our* guesses at what beans, milk and cups cost.

Our guesses are wrong. Yours won't be.

Do one thing today, it takes 90 seconds: open **Prices**, and type in what you actually pay for milk. Watch every card with milk in it re-price itself.

That number next to your latte? That's the first true thing your menu has said about money in a while.

— Portafile

P.S. Everything lives in your browser. Nothing uploaded, no account, and the Data menu exports a backup — do that once you've entered real prices.

---

## Email 2 — day 2 (skip to 3 if they haven't mentioned logging a dial-in)
**Subject: Your shop has a memory now**

You logged your first dial-in. Small thing, big deal: that grind setting used to live in whoever opened this morning.

Two habits that make the board earn its tape:
1. **Log every change**, even the boring ones ("humid, tightened 0.3"). The notes are the good part in six months.
2. When a new bag runs different, check the history before you fight the grinder from scratch — you've probably dialed this bean in before.

Your espresso cards now show the current dial-in automatically. Print fresh cards when it drifts far.

---

## Email 3 — day 4
**Subject: The app is not the product. The paper is.**

Honest admission: we don't want you looking at Portafile during a rush. Nobody unlocks a phone with wet hands at 7:58am.

Print tab → **Station sheet**. One page, your whole menu, tape it to the low-boy. Then **Recipe cards** — laminate the five drinks your newest barista still second-guesses.

The printed cards leave your costs off on purpose. The **Margin report** is the owner-only one — that one goes in the office, not on the bar.

---

## Email 4 — day 7 (send early if they mention a new hire coming)
**Subject: New hire starting? Pack ready in ten minutes.**

The next time someone new stands behind your bar, do this the night before:

1. **Training** tab — reorder the path (↑/↓ buttons) into the order you actually teach (Day 1: machine + espresso + americano is how most shops start).
2. Add them by name.
3. Print tab → **New-hire pack**: cover page with your shop's name, day-by-day checklist, every card, today's dial-ins.

Hand it over Monday 6:45am. You look like a shop that has its act together — because now you are one. Tick sign-offs as they land; the dates are your record.

Food service loses staff at 4.3% a month. The pack is why the next one doesn't take the recipes with them.

---

## Email 5 — day 12
**Subject: What the margin report is really for**

Run **Print → Margin report** and look at the right-hand column.

Most shops find one drink quietly underwater — usually something with oat milk, a syrup, and a 16oz cup that got priced in 2023. At an 11% average margin, one bad drink at volume is real money.

You have three moves, all fine: raise the price, rebuild the recipe, or keep it knowingly as a loss-leader. What's no longer on the menu: not knowing.

That's the whole product. If Portafile earned its tape spot, the Shop tier ($29/mo flat, or $199/yr — founding shops $99) adds sync and staff phones when you want it. If not, the Local version is yours forever and we mean that.

— Portafile
*Your whole shop, dialed in.*
