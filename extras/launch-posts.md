## 1. r/barista — the flagship post (day 1)
**Title: I read 200 posts across the coffee subs about shops with no recipe book / no training — so I built the thing everyone kept describing. It's free and I want you to break it.**

Hey — builder here, being upfront about that.

I went down a rabbit hole reading this sub and its neighbors: the barista whose boss told *them* to write the training guide. The shop with no recipe book "to make sure drinks are being made the same." The new hire wishing they could photograph the recipe binder before their second shift. The owner with 7 years in specialty saying 9/10 shops have no standards at all.

So I built Portafile: recipe cards that live in your browser, know what each drink costs, carry the current dial-in for the bean, and print into a new-hire pack (cover page, day-by-day checklist, cards, today's numbers). The printed cards hide costs on purpose — they're for the bar, not the office.

It's free, local-first (your data never leaves your browser), no account, and honestly I'd rather have this sub's opinion than anyone's money: what's wrong with it? What would your shop actually need before you'd tape these cards to the machine?

## 2. r/cafe / owner groups — the money angle (day 3)
**Title: I computed what a latte actually costs at 2026 prices — the "roughly $1" rule is dead. Free calculator inside (I built it).**

Green coffee peaked at $4.41/lb in 2025 — a historic high — and most of us are still pricing off numbers from before that. I built a free tool that prices every drink on your menu from what YOU pay (milk, beans, cups, syrup pumps), flags anything over your COGS target, and re-prices the whole menu when a supplier bumps you.

Seeded with 15 standard drinks so you're editing, not authoring. Runs in your browser, nothing uploaded, free. Builder here, happy to answer anything — including "why should I trust free."
