1 Setting up your shop about ten minutes

Do this on the phone or laptop you'll actually keep — see the owner device in section 4.

  1. Open portafile.vercel.app/product/.
  2. Fill in the short form. Email and shop name are required; role, city and phone are optional. It's one time per device, with no password and no confirmation email. It's how I know which shops are using this, so I can help when something breaks.
  3. You'll land in a demo shop with fifteen drinks already in it. That's a starting point, not your menu. Tap the shop name at the top and type yours.
  4. Put in what you actually pay, on the Prices tab — per gram of beans, per fluid ounce of milk, per cup, per pump of syrup. This is the step that makes everything else true.
  5. Fix up the drinks on the Recipes tab — delete what you don't serve, add what you do, and set your real menu prices.
  6. Turn on sync when you're ready to share with your team (section 3).

The starter costs are plausible guesses, not your invoices. They exist so the app opens with believable numbers instead of zeros — replace them with yours.

2 The five tabs

Recipes
Every drink as a card: what it costs you to make, what you keep, and its COGS percentage — cost of goods sold, the share of the menu price eaten by ingredients. The top of the page shows your average, how many drinks are over target, and your thinnest margin. The target is yours to set; 30% is the default. Change one ingredient price and every card re-prices immediately.
Prices
Your ingredient list: name, unit, what you pay per unit, and which drinks use it. This is the engine room. On a phone the table scrolls sideways — swipe it to reach the last columns.
Dial-in
A board per bean: grind, dose, yield, time and a note. Log it in five seconds. The most recent dial-in shows up on every recipe card using that bean, and the history stays — so when that seasonal Ethiopia comes back, you're not starting from scratch.
Training
A day-by-day path built from your drinks plus any skills you add ("closing checklist", "milk steaming"), with sign-offs and a progress bar per barista. Add and remove baristas as your team changes.
Print
Four documents made to be printed and taped where the work happens: Recipe cards (two per page, build, steps and current dial-in), Station sheet (the whole menu on one dense page), New-hire pack (cover, checklist, every card, today's dial-ins) and Margin report.

Only the margin report shows your money

The recipe cards, the station sheet and the new-hire pack never show costs or margins, so you can hand them to anyone without handing over your books. The Margin report is the one page that does: that one's for you.

3 Sharing with your staff

Everything lives only in your browser until you decide otherwise. To put it on your team's phones:

  1. Data ▾ → Turn on sync. Your shop gets a secret link.
  2. Copy link and send it to your baristas.
  3. They open it. No account, no password, nothing to install. They see the same live data, and edits show up in about 30 seconds.

When someone leaves the team, reset the link: Data ▾ → Reset staff link. The old link stops working, devices still using it have the shop's data cleared from them and show a "link turned off" screen, and you get a fresh link to send to everyone still there. Give it up to about 90 seconds to take effect. It cannot be undone, and old links never come back to life.

Two honest limits. Someone technical enough could clear their browser storage and get a blank app back — they'd have none of your data, but the lock isn't a vault. And a phone that's switched off or offline won't find out it's been cut off until it reconnects. Anyone who exported a backup before you reset still has that file.

4 Things worth knowing

The owner device. Whichever phone or laptop you used to turn on sync becomes the owner device. It's the only one that can reset the staff link. Your data is safe either way, but if you wipe that browser you lose the ability to revoke links. Pick a device you'll keep.

One shop per link. A link is one specific shop's data. Don't share the same link between two businesses — you'd both be editing the same recipes.

Two people editing at once. If you both change the same drink within a few seconds, the last change wins. At shop scale that isn't a problem.

Back it up. Data ▾ → Export backup saves a file of everything. Do it once you've finished setting up, and now and then after. Import restores it on any device.

It speaks Portuguese too

Crema follows your browser's language, so an English browser opens in English and you don't need to do anything. Next to the ? in the top right there's a picker with EN and PT. While the shop is still the untouched demo, the menu changes with the language — press PT and you get a Lisbon café's drinks in euros. Press EN to come straight back. Once the shop is yours — the moment you rename it, edit a drink or log a dial-in — switching language only ever translates the buttons and menus, and never your recipes. Those are your words.

Paper beats screens during a rush. Print the station sheet and the recipe cards. The app is for keeping them true, not for reading at the bar with milk in your hand.

5 What this costs, and your data

This costs you nothing. There's no payment, no card, no trial clock and no account to cancel. There is no billing system connected to this app at all.

About the prices on the marketing page. The tiers you'll see there — Local $0, Shop $29/mo, Roaster Partner $149/mo — are placeholders: a sketch of what pricing might be one day. They're not something you're on, not something that starts after a trial, and not something that can bill you. If that ever changes you'll hear it from me first, in plain language.

Your data. It lives in your browser, plus a cloud copy once sync is on. Your costs and margins are yours — I don't look at them, and there's no analytics inside the app. The only thing I keep is the signup form, your email and shop name, so I can reach you. Ask me and I'll delete it.

6 If something goes wrong

Export a backup first (Data ▾ → Export backup) before touching anything else. Then text or email me: what you tapped, what you expected, and what happened instead — a screenshot is perfect. If the data itself looks wrong, send me that backup file too.

This is early software, built for a handful of shops before anyone else. If something's clumsy or missing, say so — that's the reason you're getting it first.
