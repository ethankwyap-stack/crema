# Portafile — a guide for your shop

Thanks for trying this. Portafile keeps your recipes, your real costs, your espresso
dial-ins and your new-hire training in one place, on your phone and your staff's phones.
This guide covers what you're getting, how to set it up, and the few things worth knowing
before you rely on it.

**Web address:** portafile.vercel.app/product/

---

## 1. What this costs you: nothing

There is no payment, no credit card, no trial clock, and no account to cancel. Everything
described in this guide is switched on and free for you.

**About the prices on the marketing page:** if you visit the main portafile.vercel.app page
you'll see tiers — Local $0, Shop $29/mo, Roaster Partner $149/mo. **Those numbers are
placeholders.** They're a sketch of what pricing might look like one day, not something you
are on, not something that starts after a trial, and not something that can bill you. There
is no billing system connected to this app at all. If that ever changes, you'll hear it from
me first, in plain language, and nothing will start charging you by surprise.

## 2. Setting up your shop (about ten minutes)

Do this on the phone or laptop you'll actually keep — see "the owner device" in section 5.

1. **Open portafile.vercel.app/product/.**
2. **Fill in the short form** — email and shop name are required; role, city and phone are
   optional. It's one time per device, there's no password and no confirmation email. It's
   how I know which shops are using this so I can help when something breaks.
3. **You'll land in a demo shop** with fifteen drinks already in it. That's a starting point,
   not your menu. Rename it: tap the shop name at the top and type yours.
4. **Go to the Prices tab and put in what you actually pay** — per gram of beans, per fluid
   ounce of milk, per cup, per pump of syrup. This is the step that makes everything else
   true. The starter numbers are plausible guesses, not your invoices.
5. **Fix up the drinks on the Recipes tab** — delete what you don't serve, add what you do,
   and set your real menu prices.
6. **Turn on sync** when you're ready to share with staff (section 4).

## 3. The five tabs

**Recipes** — every drink as a card showing what it costs you to make, what you keep, and
its COGS percentage (cost of goods sold — the share of the menu price eaten by ingredients).
The top of the page shows your average, how many drinks are over target, and your thinnest
margin. You can set your own COGS target; 30% is the default. Change one ingredient price
and every card re-prices itself immediately.

**Prices** — your ingredient list: name, unit, what you pay per unit, and which drinks use
it. This is the engine room. On a phone the table scrolls sideways — swipe it to reach the
last columns.

**Dial-in** — a board per bean: grind, dose, yield, time, and a note. Log it in five seconds.
The most recent dial-in for a bean shows up on every recipe card that uses it, and the
history stays, so when that seasonal Ethiopia comes back you're not starting from scratch.

**Training** — build a day-by-day path from your drinks plus any custom skills ("closing
checklist", "milk steaming"), reorder it, then track sign-offs per barista with a progress
bar. Add and remove baristas as your team changes.

**Print** — four things, made to be printed and taped where the work happens:
- *Recipe cards* — two per page, build and steps and current dial-in.
- *Station sheet* — the whole menu on one dense page.
- *New-hire pack* — cover, training checklist, every card, today's dial-ins.
- *Margin report* — **owner-only.** This is the one page that shows costs and margins.

Recipe cards, the station sheet and the new-hire pack deliberately **never show your costs**,
so you can hand them to anyone without handing over your margins.

## 4. Sharing with your staff

Everything lives in your browser until you choose otherwise. To put it on your team's phones:

1. **Data menu → Turn on sync.** Your shop gets a secret link.
2. **Data menu → Copy link**, and send that link to your baristas.
3. They open it. No account, no password, no app to install. They see the same live data,
   and their edits show up on your phone within about thirty seconds.

**When someone leaves, reset the link.** Data menu → **Reset staff link**. This does three
things: the old link stops working, every device still using it gets the shop's data erased
from it and shows a "this link has been turned off" screen, and you get a fresh link to send
to the people who are still there. Give it up to about ninety seconds to take effect on their
devices. It cannot be undone, and old links never come back to life.

**Two honest limits on that.** Someone technical enough could clear their browser's storage
to get a blank app back — they'd have none of your data, but the lock isn't a vault. And a
phone that's switched off or offline won't find out it's been cut off until it reconnects.
If someone exported a backup file before you reset the link, they still have that file.

## 5. Things worth knowing

**The owner device.** Whichever phone or laptop you used to turn on sync becomes the owner
device. It's the only one that can reset the staff link. Your data is safe regardless — it's
in the cloud copy and on the other devices — but if you wipe that browser you lose the
ability to revoke links. Pick a device you'll keep, and don't set up your shop on a device
you're about to replace.

**One shop per link.** A link is one specific shop's data. Don't share a link between two
businesses — you'd both be editing the same recipes.

**Two people editing at once.** If two of you change the same drink within a few seconds of
each other, the last change wins. It's not a problem at shop scale, but it's worth knowing
that this isn't built for a dozen people editing simultaneously.

**Back it up.** Data menu → **Export backup** saves a file of everything. Do it after you
finish your initial setup, and now and then afterwards. Keep it with your shop documents.
**Import** restores it on any device.

**Your data.** It lives in your browser, plus a cloud copy once sync is on. Your ingredient
costs and margins are yours; I don't look at them and there's no analytics on the app. The
only thing I keep is the signup form — your email and shop name — so I can reach you.
Ask me any time and I'll delete it.

**It speaks Portuguese too.** Portafile follows your *browser's* language, not your country,
so an English browser opens in English and you don't have to do anything. Next to the **?**
in the top right there's a picker with **EN** and **PT**. Two things are worth knowing about
it. While your shop is still the untouched demo, the *menu* changes with the language —
press PT and you get a Lisbon café's drinks priced in euros, in millilitres and °C; press EN
and you're straight back to the fifteen American ones. But **once the shop is yours** — the
moment you rename it, edit a drink, change a price or log a dial-in — switching language only
ever translates the buttons and menus, and **never your recipes**. Those are your words, and
nothing rewrites them. Language is part of the shop rather than the device, so if you turn on
sync, your staff's phones follow whichever you picked.

**Printing beats screens during a rush.** Print the station sheet and the recipe cards. The
app is for keeping them true, not for reading at the bar with milk in your hand.

## 6. If something breaks

Text or email me. Tell me what you tapped, what you expected, and what happened instead —
a screenshot is perfect. If something looks wrong with your data, **export a backup first**,
before touching anything else, and send me that file.

This is early software, built for two shops before anyone else. If something's clumsy or
missing, say so — that's the reason you're getting it first.
